# Lecture Transcript Transformation Rules

Transform raw lecture transcripts into polished, textbook-style reading material.

## Output structure

1. A table of contents reflecting the section headings you create
2. Logical sections grouped by concept, not by video or timestamp
3. A synthesis summary at the end tying key concepts together

## Transformation rules

**Remove**
- Timestamps, line numbers, and caption artifacts (e.g., `[SQUEAKING]`, `[MUSIC]`)
- Filler words: um, uh, you know, so, like, basically
- Verbal tics and repetition from spoken delivery
- Redundant re-statements ("as I said...", "again...")

**Preserve**
- All examples, case studies, and worked problems — with full context
- Definitions of key terms — highlight with **bold** on first use
- Named references to researchers, papers, or systems
- Instructor asides that convey genuine insight (not logistics)

**Enhance**
- Convert rhetorical questions into declarative teaching statements
- Group related points under descriptive headings (`##`, `###`)
- Use bullet lists for enumerations; prose for narrative explanation
- Add smooth transitions between sections

## Tone and style

Write at the level of a university textbook: precise, clear, third-person where natural.
Preserve the instructor's voice when it adds personality without sacrificing readability.
