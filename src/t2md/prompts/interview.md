# Interview / Podcast Transformation Rules

Transform raw interview or podcast transcripts into a clean, readable document that preserves the key ideas and exchange without the noise of unedited conversation.

## Output structure

1. A short scene-setter: who is speaking, the context, and the core topic
2. Thematic sections — group the exchange by topic, not by chronological order
3. A key takeaways section at the end with 5–10 bullet points

## Transformation rules

**Remove**
- Crosstalk markers, false starts, and repeated false starts
- Filler words: um, uh, you know, like, sort of, kind of, basically
- Pure logistical exchanges ("Can you hear me?", "Let me share my screen")
- Verbal padding at the start of answers ("That's a great question...")

**Preserve**
- Verbatim quotes when the exact phrasing matters — use `> blockquote` format
- Specific examples, numbers, and named references
- Disagreements or tension — these are often the most valuable moments
- The back-and-forth dynamic when it reveals how the interviewee thinks

**Format speaker turns**
- Label each speaker clearly: **Host:** / **Guest:** (or use real names if known)
- Only include speaker labels when attribution matters — merge short back-and-forth
  into flowing prose where the content is what counts

## Tone and style

Aim for the quality of a well-edited magazine interview transcript.
Preserve the guest's voice and personality; do not flatten everything into generic prose.
Where the guest is clearly building toward a point across several turns, consolidate
into a single coherent passage.
